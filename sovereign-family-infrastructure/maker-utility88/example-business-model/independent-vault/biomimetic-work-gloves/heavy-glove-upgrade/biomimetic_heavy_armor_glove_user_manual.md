## 🛠️ SECTION 1: THE INITIAL POST-PRINT HARVEST & MODULAR LOCK ASSEMBLY

Because these heavy thermal work gloves utilize a modular, dual-chassis system comprising an inner tactile thermal liner and an auxiliary 4.5mm glass-reinforced carapace shell, the operator must execute this strict assembly routine to lock the components together safely:

### 1. The Sub-35°C Plate Stabilization Cool-Down
*   **Action:** Allow the print chamber and the spring steel PEI sheet to naturally cool completely below 35°C before harvesting. Because the glass-sphere compounded carapace and the soft 85A TPU inner liner possess completely different material density profiles, cooling the plate under full magnetic compression forces the interlocking layer lines to cure in a state of absolute, zero-warp dimensional alignment.

### 2. The Modular Carapace Snap-On Engagement
*   **Action:** Take the harvested inner liner and place it flat against a clean wooden workbench block with the male snap-anchor posts pointing straight upward. Take the 4.5mm glass-reinforced heavy carapace shell, align its four female reciprocating snap capture slots directly over the liner's anchor posts, and apply downward thumb pressure over each node.
*   **The Mechanical Engagement:** Push firmly until you hear a loud, crisp mechanical snap at each corner. The tight, sub-micron compression ring will stretch open locally and clamp flatly over the locking ridges of the anchor posts. The glass-sphere particle compounding locks the assembly dead-flat, forming an unyielding mechanical connection that cannot slide, wiggle, or delaminate under high-temperature industrial shock waves.

### 3. The Hardware Magnet Press-Fit Insertion
*   **Action:** Source standard, high-strength **8mm x 2mm N52 Neodymium disc magnets**. Align an individual magnet disc directly over the compression slots on the finger pads or back shields, and press down firmly using flat thumb pressure. The hardcoded 0.20mm tolerance compression reduction ring will collar the chamfered rim of the magnet with an airtight physical lock, providing a permanent physical collar without any messy shop glues, epoxies, or metal clips.

## 🔬 SECTION 2: HIGH-TEMPERATURE FIELD OPERATIONAL LIMITS

To maintain absolute operator hand safety and prevent physical tool damage, the workshop cell must enforce these strict field operating boundaries:

### 1. The 220°C Thermal Saturation Cap
*   **The Action:** The *Desert Silver Ant* prismatic micro-grooves and dead-air wells provide phenomenal insulation. However, the maximum continuous contact threshold for direct high-heat handling is capped at exactly **220°C (428°F)**. Do not exceed this temperature limit or submerge the glove into open slag furnaces, as the polyurethane base elastomer will approach its soft melting window, compromising the structural integrity of the internal leaf valve boundaries.

### 2. The Post-Heat Mechanical Wipe Routine
*   **The Action:** Following high-temperature extrusion piping adjustments or wet welding shifts, wash the glove palm under warm water mixed with standard liquid dish soap. **Always wipe your hand in a single direction from the wrist forward toward the fingertips.** This glides down the natural lay of the fluid-channeling chevron ribs, flushing away trapped shop grease, grit, or hot scale particles through the deep multi-axis gutters. Never rub the glove backward, as this fights the directional friction matrix and can blunt the scale ridges.
