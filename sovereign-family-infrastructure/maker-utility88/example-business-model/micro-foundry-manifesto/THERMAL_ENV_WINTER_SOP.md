# 🏛️ Micro-Foundry Infrastructure SOP: Winter Thermal Retention Protocols
**Zoning Node:** Stealth Residential Garage Cell Integration (Westland, MI Corridor)  
**System Core:** Mitigating Interlayer Delamination & Thermoplastic Die-Swell Shocks  
**Hardware Enclosure Profile:** 3/4-inch High-Mass OSB Cabinet Array + 2-inch Polyurethane Insulation  

Operating an industrial 3D-printing fleet in an unheated or partially insulated Michigan garage environment introduces a severe material failure vector: Ambient Draft Contraction. If flexible Thermoplastic Polyurethane (TPU) layers cool down too rapidly from cold room drafts, they undergo thermal shrinkage, destroying the layer-to-layer chemical fusion and causing the components to fracture under stride tension. This document hardcodes the exact mechanical setups and automated switches to turn your soundproofing boxes into high-efficiency thermal climate chambers.

---

## 📈 1. THE THERMAL RETENTION ENCLOSURE ARCHITECTURE

Rather than running expensive commercial garage space heaters that draw high currents and inflate your household utility bills, our model traps the natural heat generated by the printers themselves inside the insulated wood shell.

### 🔹 Component 1: The High-Mass Insulation Shell
*   **The Blueprint:** Every stacked utility rack housing your print engines is completely enclosed in our hard-coded 3/4-inch OSB wood panelling and lined internally with 2-inch thick pyramid polyurethane acoustic foam. This dual-layer matrix serves an essential secondary purpose: it acts as a high-grade thermal insulation blanket with a massive R-value rating.

### 🔹 Component 2: The Free Mechanical Heat Source
*   **The Physics:** You do not install electric heating coils inside the boxes. A single CoreXY print bed heated to 55°C, combined with an active 238°C toolhead nozzle, naturally exhausts continuous radiant heat waves into the cabinet. 
*   **The Compounding Effect:** Running multiple machines simultaneously inside the closed wood cabinets automatically raises the internal box temperature to a stable, balmy **95°F to 105°F (35°C to 40°C) within twenty minutes** of flipping the power keys, completely neutralizing the freezing room air outside.

### 🔹 Component 3: The Automated Inkbird Switch Node (The Circuit Patch)
*   **The Problem:** If you leave your 4-inch inline ventilation fans running continuously during a winter blizzard, they will draw the cold room air into the box far too quickly, shocking the plastic.
*   **The Sourcing Fix:** Wire the exhaust fan power lines directly into an off-the-shelf **Inkbird ITC-308 Digital Temperature Controller** ($35.00 counter item sourced via your open industrial supply lines).
*   **The Automated Logic:** 
    *   Place the digital temperature probe inside the central cabinet air space. 
    *   Set the target high-temperature activation threshold to precisely **40°C (104°F)**.
    *   When the print sequence initiates, the inline fans stay completely dead, allowing the internal box environment to rapidly warm up and lock in your layer-line boundary densities.
    *   The exact micro-second the internal box temperature hits 40°C, the controller triggers the circuit, turning the inline fans on a low-velocity micro-purge to exhaust out the rubber smells while maintaining a perfectly constant, warm, draft-free printing atmosphere.

## 🛠️ 2. SUB-ZERO WINTER PRINT RUN ADJUSTMENT MATRIX

When the exterior Westland weather drop falls below freezing, your direct-to-consumer print jobs must be dispatched with these updated firmware variables to maximize layer-weld fusion:

1.  **Enforce 0% Part Cooling Fans:** Inside your `footwear_slicing_profiles.json`, keep the toolhead part cooling fan curves locked to flatly zero across the entire Layer 1 and Layer 2 footprints. Forcing internal print fans to spin in winter introduces a minor air swirl that fractures the rubber chain lengths.
2.  **Elevate Nozzle Temperatures by +5°C:** Push your extrusion melting profiles to the upper chemical limit to lower viscosity and expand the molecular bonding window:
    *   *85A TPU Uppers:* Increase from 232°C up to **237°C**.
    *   *95A TPU Cushion Soles:* Increase from 238°C up to **243°C**.
3.  **Implement a 10-Minute Pre-Heat Delay:** Before dispatching an overnight production G-code track, wirelessly turn on all heated beds to 55°C and leave the enclosure doors sealed for 10 minutes. This pre-charges the internal air envelope to room-temperature parameters, ensuring Layer 1 adheres perfectly to the textured PEI plates without early thermal contraction.
