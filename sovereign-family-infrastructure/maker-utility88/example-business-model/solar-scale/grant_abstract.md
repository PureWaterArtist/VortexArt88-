# 🏛️ Project Proposal Grant Abstract: Distributed Micro-Foundry Infrastructure

### A. Project Objective & Strategic State Alignment
Matrix Biomimetic Utilities, LLC is establishing an automated, localized Circular Micro-Foundry Pilot Unit in Westland, Michigan. This facility utilizes advanced additive engineering and motorized polymer reclamation systems to manufacture multi-layered, scale-invariant infrastructure components entirely from post-consumer recycled plastics. 

Our core portfolio directly addresses the strategic pillars of the **Michigan Department of Environment, Great Lakes, and Energy (EGLE)** and the **MEDC**:
1.  **The Solar-Scale Engine:** A 24-hour energy harvesting asset combining angle-invariant micro-prisms with solid-state Seebeck thermoelectric harvesters to provide decentralized, parallel grid resilience that operates day and night without centralized infrastructure dependencies.
2.  **The Strata-Flow Tree:** A passive, gravity-fed greywater diversion unit leveraging centrifugal fluid dynamics and anti-bacterial sharkskin textures to purify domestic drainage on-site, directly supporting Great Lakes watershed water conservation mandates.

### B. Technical Feasibility & Asset Verification
The entire structural ecosystem is fully mature, open-source, and version-controlled via public parametric CAD engines and C++ automation firmware architectures. By executing production through a redundant cell of enclosed high-speed CoreXY print engines running specialized, watertight multi-material layer settings, the facility operates at zero material waste. The design requires zero complex tooling dies or foreign injection-molded components, allowing localized supply chains to pivot on-demand via pure digital instructions.

### C. Public Impact & Circular Milestones
Grant funding will be allocated 100% toward the acquisition of physical capital hardware assets (motorized shredders, desktop filament extruders, and enclosed production units). By operating under a Lifetime Circular Warranty, this project creates an active, community-driven collection network. Worn or retired units are retrieved via prepaid logistics loops, shredded, re-compounded, and remanufactured locally—establishing an unassailable, scalable template for regional small-business circular economics within the State of Michigan.
