## 💧 Navigating Around Obstacles Like Water Around a Rock

In full-scale aerospace engineering, carving thousands of large 2.2mm water tracks into heavy glass creates a flight computer that weighs several tons. That weight budget makes it incredibly hard to build a giant prototype without a massive corporate budget.

**We navigate around this problem cleanly by scaling the entire design down into a lightweight, 350mm desktop RC drone.**

When you shrink the physical size of the vehicle down to the size of a standard hobby drone, the rules of nature completely change in our favor:
*   **The Micro-Tracks:** We shrink the water channels down from 2.2mm to a microscopic **250 micrometers** (the width of a few human hairs). 
*   **The Weight Erased:** Because the tracks are microscopic, the entire glass computer shrinks down to the size of a business card and weighs a tiny **42.5 grams**. It easily fits on a small drone body.
*   **Super Smooth Flow:** In small channels, water naturally stops swirling and churning. It enters a state called **ultra-laminar flow**, where surface tension keeps the liquid water rope perfectly stable, completely preventing steering delays or bubble lockups.

## 🛠️ Step-by-Step Benchtop Assembly Protocol

You can easily build this first working proof-of-concept right on your home workbench using non-proprietary parts:

### 🖨️ Step 1: Print the Transparent Shell
Load the 350mm model into a standard desktop **SLA Resin 3D Printer** (like an Elegoo Mars or Formlabs Form 4). Print the wavy 1:1.618 spin-discs and microfluidic logic housing out of tough, transparent photopolymer resin. This allows you to visually watch the water vortex loop move inside the channels with your own eyes. Spray the internal grooves with a commercial aerosolized non-stick coating to enforce the 98% super-slip floor.

### 🔌 Step 2: Mount the Hobby Motor
Bolt a standard **1400KV brushless hobby drone motor** directly into the center coaxial zero-contact drive hub. Connect the motor to a standard RC speed controller (ESC) and a 4S 1300mAh high-discharge LiPo battery.

### 📥 Step 3: Inject the Water Charge
Take a standard cleanroom syringe and inject exactly **350 milliliters of pure distilled water** into the closed loop tracking lines. Lightly tap the frame to clear out any visible air pockets to ensure absolute hydraulic logic continuity.

### 🕹️ Step 4: The Static Takeoff
Place the drone on your test pad and clear a 2-meter boundary space. Push the throttle stick on your RC transmitter forward. The brushless motor will instantly deliver a high-torque spin, driving the wavy discs past the critical **12,500 RPM ignition point** in less than 1.2 seconds. The air inside the grooves cools rapidly to 4°C, tearing open a severe vacuum above the craft that cleanly lifts the 1.85 kg drone into a stable hover.
